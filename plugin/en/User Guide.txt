================================================================
  Local Quad Retopo (LocalQuadRetopo) - User Guide    Version 1.4.3
================================================================

For installation, first read "README.txt" in the same folder. This
guide explains how to use the plug-in.


1. What it does
----------------------------------------------------------------

Mask an area of a model in ZBrush and the plug-in rebuilds that area
as quads. Faces outside the mask stay exactly as they were, and the
new and old parts meet point for point along the mask border, with
no gaps and no overlaps.

Typical uses:
  - triangle areas left behind by Sculptris Pro or DynaMesh
  - faces stretched long and thin by a brush
  - one area you are not happy with after a full ZRemesher pass,
    when you want to redo only that area


2. The panel
----------------------------------------------------------------

Location: ZPlugin > Local Quad Retopo

  Target Faces - 0 Auto
      Roughly how many faces the masked area has after the retopo.
      0 = decided from the face density around the mask, so the new
      faces are about the size of their neighbours.
      Dragging moves in steps of 10; click the slider to type a
      number. Range 0-5000.

  Recommend Faces
      Reads the current mask, puts the recommended face count in the
      slider and shows it at the top. Useful when you want to see a
      number first and then decide to go higher or lower.

  Retopo Mask - Regular
      Keeps the regular quads that ZRemesher creates in the middle
      and adds a ring of transition faces between them and the mask
      border. Clean edge flow.

  Retopo Mask - Honeycomb
      Rebuilds the whole area as quads of even size, with a
      honeycomb-like edge flow. Does not use ZRemesher, is faster,
      and works for almost any mask shape.

  Back to Original
      Switches back to the model from before the run (for the most
      recent run only).


3. Basic use
----------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Select the SubTool to work on and make sure it is in Edit mode
     (the Edit button is lit).
  2. Hold Ctrl and paint a mask over the area to retopologize.
     - The mask may be several separate pieces and may span several
       polygroups.
     - A mask slightly larger than the problem area gives better
       results.
  3. Click "Retopo Mask - Regular" or "Retopo Mask - Honeycomb".
  4. Wait a few seconds to a little over ten seconds. ZBrush does
     not respond during this time; that is normal. Do not click
     anything else.
  5. When it is done:
     - The result is a new SubTool. The original model stays in the
       SubTool list, hidden.
     - If you do not like it, click "Back to Original", adjust the
       mask or the face count and run again.
     - Once you are happy, you can delete the original SubTool
       yourself.

Leave the target face count at 0 in most cases. For a denser or
sparser result, click "Recommend Faces" to get a starting number,
raise or lower it, then run.


4. Choosing a layout
----------------------------------------------------------------

                 Regular                       Honeycomb
  Look       a tidy grid in the middle,    even face size,
             a ring of transition faces    honeycomb-like flow
             at the border
  Speed      slower (about 5-25 s)         faster (about 2-15 s)
  Reliability depends on ZRemesher; may    succeeds almost always
             fail on very narrow or
             scattered masks
  Best for   areas you will subdivide,     areas that only need
             keep sculpting, or where      even, unstretched faces,
             edge flow matters             or when Regular fails

When the regular layout cannot produce a valid result, the plug-in
switches to the honeycomb layout by itself (areas that were already
clean quads are "paired" instead, which keeps their edge flow), so
the run does not fail because of it. When the masked area passes
through itself a lot, the plug-in goes straight to the honeycomb
layout and says so at the top.


5. Recommended workflow with Sculptris Pro
----------------------------------------------------------------

Sculptris Pro adds faces while you sculpt, so no amount of pulling
leaves stretched faces; the cost is that every brushed area turns
into triangles. This plug-in turns those areas back into quads.
Alternate between the two:

  1. Turn Sculptris Pro on and sculpt freely, without worrying
     about topology.
  2. When one part is done, turn Sculptris Pro off.
  3. Mask the area that became triangles. Make the mask slightly
     larger than the triangle area so a little of the clean quads
     around it is included; the join looks more natural.
  4. Click one of the two "Retopo Mask" buttons to turn the area
     back into quads.
  5. Move on to the next part and repeat.

You sculpt without topology limits, and the model stays mostly quads,
ready for subdividing, smoothing and UV unwrapping.

Tip: give the button you use most a hotkey, and the workflow becomes
"paint the mask, press a key". In ZBrush, hold Ctrl+Alt and click the
button, then press the key you want. Save it with
Preferences > Hotkeys > Store so it is kept after a restart.


6. Things to know
----------------------------------------------------------------

  - Save your project before using the regular layout. It calls
    ZBrush's own ZRemesher, which can crash ZBrush on a few extreme
    meshes; the plug-in cannot prevent that. The honeycomb layout
    does not call ZRemesher.
  - With a very dense mask border, or large faces around it, the
    final face count is higher than the target, because both sides
    of the border need enough faces to join.
  - If faces inside the masked area already pass through each other
    (for example a face pulled into another one with the Move
    brush), a few crossings may remain after the retopo. A new
    layout cannot change the shape; fix the shape first.
  - The regular layout softens hard creases inside the mask a little.
  - "Recommend Faces" and both "Retopo Mask" buttons show any parts
    of the model you had hidden, and turn the mask into a hard-edged
    mask (soft falloff is lost). This does not affect the retopo.
  - A mask is required, and it must cover only part of the SubTool.
    With no mask, or a mask over the whole SubTool, the plug-in
    shows a message at the top and stops without changing anything.
    The mask is gone after a retopo; to work on the same area again,
    paint the mask again.
  - Every run adds one result SubTool; remove original models you no
    longer need.
  - Do not mask too large an area at once. Ten thousand faces take
    over ten seconds; a run that takes more than about a minute
    counts as failed. Split the mask into several parts then.
  - These models are refused: models with UVs, materials, explicit
    normals or vertex colour (Polypaint). Work on a copy without
    them if needed.


7. When a run fails
----------------------------------------------------------------

A failed run shows "Check failed" and the path of a report file at
the top. The model is put back as it was before the run and is not
damaged. Common causes and fixes:

  - "No usable mask": paint a mask first, and do not cover the
    whole SubTool.
  - The mask is too small or too narrow: make it larger, at least a
    dozen or so faces.
  - Where the mask border touches itself diagonally at one point,
    the plug-in leaves the few faces around that point outside the
    mask, unchanged, and rebuilds the rest. Usually nothing needs to
    be done. If a run still fails because of it, adjust the mask so
    its border is a simple closed loop.
  - The regular layout fails: use the honeycomb layout.
  - Repeated failures: send the report file (the file in C:\LQR
    ending in _report.txt) to the plug-in author.
